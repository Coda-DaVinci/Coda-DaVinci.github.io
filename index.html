Code as a guide for video generation

Coding agents can build executable 3D scenes to guide video generation. This gives us control over scene structure and physical events, but modeling every detail can also introduce stiff motion. The key is deciding how to combine the strengths of coding agents and video models.

We study modeling granularity, physics simulation, and synthesis prompts through controlled experiments. These findings lead to Coda-DaVinci: a task-adaptive framework that selects when to use 3D modeling, assigns each entity an appropriate level of detail, and binds the resulting proxy to a synthesis prompt. A persistent scene workspace and visual feedback support revision and long-video continuation.

A task-adaptive Code2Video framework

Coda-DaVinci uses explicit 3D scenes when the task needs precise control. It favors direct generation for natural body movement when a proxy offers little benefit. For modeled scenes, environments and objects receive structured geometry, while human proxies stay coarse. The coding agent inspects the proxy and writes a prompt that binds its subjects to their intended appearance and actions.

Route by task

Use a scene program where explicit control helps; choose direct synthesis when natural motion is the priority.

Model selectively

Keep structured environments and objects, while leaving fine human articulation to the video model.

Ground the prompt

Link subject descriptions to proxy entities and appearance references before synthesis.

What makes Code2Video work?

Our controlled study finds that coarse proxies preserve more natural motion on motion-intensive tasks, while medium-grained geometry supports scene consistency. Physics simulation and proxy-grounded prompts further improve average quality.

Blender achieves higher scores than Three.js on the diagnostic subset, at roughly twice the agent token cost. Long-video experiments show stronger event structure and scene consistency, with remaining challenges in transition stability and adherence to scene settings.
